How it is modeled
  • Each joint has one line. Stroke in millimeters is the joint's moment arm times its angle.
  • The fingertip joint follows the middle joint at two thirds of its angle, so it needs no line of its own.
  • Tension at the motor is tension at the finger times e to the power (friction times total bend). Total bend is about 0.95 rad of routing, plus the wrist angle, plus the knuckle angle for tubes that cross a knuckle.
  • Pushing uses 1.6 times the friction of pulling, because beads drag sideways. The push limit is where the bead column would buckle.
  • Joint ranges, moment arms, finger lengths, forces and cord strength are rounded textbook-level estimates, not measurements. Change them under Assumptions. Beads are drawn oversize so they are visible.

Tendon, motors in the forearm

Tesla, 1X, Foundation, Kyber, Daxo

BetLight human-size fingers, all the power in the forearm.

ReportedOptimus Gen 3 uses 25 actuators, up from 17 [2]. Foundation uses magnetic sensors for finger position and caught a baseball [2]. Daxo runs up to 120 tendons and motors, and 1X about 22 DOF [9].

Open problemFriction in the routing tubes hurts control and wears the strings, so these hands need extra sensing [2]. Three patent assignees still treat slack as unsolved [3].

Direct drive, motors in the fingers

Wuji, Sharpa, Figure's current direction

BetNo cables, so no friction and very predictable control.

ReportedWuji Hand 2: 20 DOF, 0.6 kg, 10 kg payload, $16,000, in production [4]. Sharpa: 22 DOF in mass production [2]. The talk says sim-to-real gap is almost zero for these hands [9].

Open problemFinger volume limits heat and torque, and motors run close to capacity [2].

Linkage and hybrids

AGILINK and others

BetRigid links push and pull, so one motor can grip and open.

ReportedA hand can have 15 joints on only 5 motors, so linkages fix part of the motion [10]. AGILINK builds both tendon and direct-drive hands [2].

Open problemCoupled motion means fingers can miss an object, and links are hard to route through a wrist [9].

Fluidic tendon

Tacta

BetFluid-filled lines drive pistons, so length changes do not matter and the wrist is easy.

Reported15 DOF, 25 N per fingertip, mechanism life up to 30 million cycles, touch sensing from 250 Pa to 700 kPa, first shipments early 2027. No price or funding was disclosed [1]. One source describes five fingers at 3 DOF each and a social post describes three fingers, so check the spec sheet.

Open problemThe actuator base is bulky today [9], and the lifetime figure is the vendor's own.

Woven tendon

Allonic

BetBraid the whole hand around a printed skeleton and treat it as a cheap, replaceable part.

ReportedMK2: 15 DOF, 0.45 kg, 3 kg strength, listed at $2,799, still a prototype, $7.2 million pre-seed in 2026 [5].

Open problemNo durability figures, and it still needs learned control [9].

Beadline

Bead-and-cord line in a tube

BetOne push-pull line per joint, 17 lines, all motors in the forearm. Pull carries the grip and the bead column carries the light opening push.

StatusA model only. Every number in the Simulator is an assumption. The closest prior art I found is a hand with linkages and push-pull cables [8], which I did not read.

Open problemWhether a bead column survives a wrist bend at finger scale, and whether friction stays stable over life. The Test plan isolates each question.

What the field still lacks
  • Independent cycle-life data. Only Tacta states a figure, and it is the vendor's [1].
  • Friction models that follow accumulated bend. One recent paper on tendon-sheath mechanisms fits friction to an exponential in bend angle, like this simulator, and reports an 88% tracking-error cut from compensating it [6].
  • A standard way to manage slack and pretension [3].
  • Routing geometry that lowers friction. The patent scan calls this white space [3].
  • A fiber muscle that is hand-ready. Nylon-based muscles reach 28.4 MPa isometric stress, far above skeletal muscle, but the review points to arms and grippers as the nearest uses, not hands [7].

Get the files

The zip holds the report, the run as CSV, a parts list, three OpenSCAD files (finger rig, bend jigs, tube clamp) and make_stl.sh.

Print and build
  • Install OpenSCAD (version 2019.05 or newer), unzip the kit, and run sh make_stl.sh. STL files land in the out folder.
  • Print in PLA or PETG at 0.2 mm layers with 4 perimeters. Lay each finger part on a side face so the fork plates print flat.
  • Ream the pin holes with a drill of the pin size. Seat two tubes in the base sockets, thread a cord through each, run it through the horn or guide post, and tie off.
  • Drive each cord with a small linear actuator and read force with a load cell at the actuator. Use the bend jigs for tests P2 and P4 and the clamp for P1.
  • I have not test-printed these parts or rendered them in OpenSCAD. Check clearances on your printer and expect to adjust.
